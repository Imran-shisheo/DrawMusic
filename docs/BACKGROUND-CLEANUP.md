# Safe startup background reclamation

Implemented for review finding 9, 27 October 2026. This is an original local storage policy, not a reconstruction of a reference app. It replaces the previous unconditional retain-all policy for **provably unreferenced, old, app-owned background PNGs**. It does not expire projects, rewrite documents, purge recovery data, scan the photo library or touch shared/public exports.

## Lifecycle and concurrency contract

`main()` now awaits `cleanupBackgroundsBeforeApplication()` after Flutter binding initialization and **before** `runApp`, ProviderScope, editor routes, document/history ownership or pickers can exist. Then `BackgroundStorageCoordinator.application.applicationStarted()` permanently prevents any further Dart cleanup in that process. There is no timer, recurring background worker or cleanup while drawing. Tests/runtime entry points calling main must await its Future.

The same pure-Dart coordinator guards every serialized mutation in FileProjectRepository and FileExportLibrary, including save, duplicate, restore, raw-recovery export and export snapshot publication. A writer already executing denies cleanup. A mutation queued during cleanup waits; its presence cancels collection and restores valid staged assets before the writer resumes. The coordinator is released in finally even after analysis/I/O/native failures. Existing reads are unaffected, but the only production cleanup entry point runs before any UI/provider can construct an in-memory editor or Undo state.

A native `beginCleanup` / `endCleanup` token lease excludes photo picking, recovery and acknowledgement during the scan/stage/sweep. Android refuses a lease for an active or restored picker/Activity, an import/source/capture/launch record, malformed/unknown picker preferences, pending callback, active video job or previous startup attempt. Completed, validated but unacknowledged imports return explicit asset-ID pins. A late external activity result is deferred until the lease ends. Picker launch state is persisted before leaving the Activity, and source/ready metadata remains protected across worker completion. Native video jobs are checked because a native encoder can survive Dart hot restart.

The iOS source has equivalent pending/active/ready checks and a video-job callback. Both native implementations **seal startup permanently after the first attempted lease** on that native engine. Dart hot restart cannot create a fresh collector against old native handlers/jobs. Native calls are token-owned/idempotent; finally retries release if its completion is lost. A broken native bridge causes conservative retention and may require engine restart; it does not authorize sweeping. iOS source is uncompiled/unverified until Mac validation; Linux tests do not establish UIKit/UserDefaults behavior.

## Complete reference marking and fail-closed decisions

The collector marks every supported revision under app-private `projects/`, including current/older retained revisions, duplicates, valid interrupted `.json.tmp` revisions, `.preserved-*` originals and soft-deleted/Undo projects. Every envelope checksum, document identity, schema/engine and known metadata shape must be understood. Unknown IDs, extension metadata, unknown background kinds, corrupt/future/partial documents, unexplained entities, symlinks or analysis-budget exhaustion close collection for **all** assets. Empty/unreadable project directories are uncertain, not evidence of zero references.

Export-library snapshots are independent reference roots: the collector scans `getApplicationDocumentsDirectory()/exports` metadata, including deleted-with-Undo snapshots, preserved metadata and valid temps. Their stored `preview` is marked even when the originating project is gone or has since changed. Encoded media files are never deleted or rewritten by cleanup. Checksummed raw-recovery bundles in both supported private recovery directories are also analyzed; their per-file original bytes/checksums/known revision metadata must be understood. An undecodable recovery bundle retains all assets rather than making a false orphan assumption.

The source inventory is fingerprinted and checked again before staging and immediately before each mature deletion. An unexpected reference/source change cancels collection. Inputs are bounded to 10,000 inspected entries, 64 MiB of analyzed bytes and a ten-second normal-analysis budget; reaching a bound retains data instead of truncating the reference set. Abort recovery has a separate two-second / 1,000-entry budget and performs only nondestructive private renames into absent active paths, without reading/hashing whole images. It never overwrites an existing active copy. Remaining staged files are retained with `recoveryDeferred:true`. The shared Flutter reader and native video readers can use such staged IDs on demand with their normal canonical-path, size, SHA and image-dimension checks; this is read-only access, not a claim that staged bytes are valid art. No physical startup-time or memory/FPS target is claimed.

## Two age-protected stages

1. An unreferenced content-addressed PNG must be at least **seven days old**. Future timestamps retain age protection. Its ID, owned path/type, size and SHA-256 must validate; invalid or unknown asset entries close collection.
2. The collector writes/flushed an owned `.cleanup-staging/<id>.json.tmp` journal, atomically publishes it as `<id>.json`, then atomically renames the PNG into that same staging directory. This preserves the only copy rather than immediately deleting it. No document refers to a staged candidate under the complete verified inventory.
3. On a **later safe startup**, the original `stagedAt` is preserved. A stage must age an **additional seven days** before deletion, followed by fresh reference verification and content-hash verification. Repeated young-stage startups do not reset its age. Both thresholds are conservative grace periods, not a storage quota.
4. Any now-pinned staged PNG is restored to active background storage. If an identical active copy was republished meanwhile, the staged duplicate is removed only after validating the active copy. Uncertain marking attempts bounded, nondestructive restoration of owned regular staged PNGs while preserving corrupt/unknown journals. Restoring bytes does not validate them: normal readers still verify the hash, size and image data. Unprocessed or conflicting copies remain available in staging and are never swept.

Journal publication precedes the PNG rename. Interrupted temporary journals without moved PNGs can be discarded as incomplete metadata; valid temporary journals with a moved PNG are completed. A PNG whose journal is absent or corrupt can be moved back using its constrained content-addressed basename without trusting or validating its image contents, and collection stays closed for that pass. Work exceeding the bounded recovery budget is deferred to on-demand readers or a later safe startup. A crash after a verified deletion but before removing its known journal is safe to reconcile on a later startup. Unknown journal versions/contents are retained and continue to close collection, rather than being silently rewritten. These are process-interruption safeguards, not an untested guarantee against arbitrary filesystem/power failure.

## Retention scope and tradeoffs

The existing repository supports restore of soft-deleted projects beyond the short UI Undo window and retains recovery revisions. Cleanup therefore keeps **all references in those recoverable project files**, rather than silently imposing a new expiration policy. Permanently removing project records is a separate operation/product policy. This collector reclaims canceled/uncommitted imports and images no longer referenced by any supported durable/pending root; it does not promise to reclaim every deleted project's storage.

Uncertain files intentionally prevent reclamation until their underlying issue is resolved; this can retain extra storage. Private recovery bundles and successful public/exported artifacts are not deleted. External photo/camera sources are outside the collector's roots. No photo image is re-encoded or project schema migrated by cleanup. No asset ID changes are required.
